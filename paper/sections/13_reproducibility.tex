\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

This appendix is the deliberate counterpart to \S\ref{sec:original}: where that
section catalogues what \citet{lagasio2024} leaves unspecified, this one
discloses every corresponding element of our own pipeline at the same level
of detail, because a paper that audits another study's reproducibility and
then reports its own loosely would be self-refuting.

\subsection{Pipeline and intermediate artefacts}

stage's output file and writing its own; there is no step that reads back

\subsection{Determinism}
\label{sec:repro-determinism}

Extraction, preprocessing and measurement together are a pure function of
the PDFs and the lexicon files in Table~\ref{tab:repro-pipeline}: no network
call, no external API, and no random seed anywhere on this path. The one
non-trivial control-flow step, the deduplication that drops \texttt{results.csv}
from 344 to 343 rows, is itself an MD5 hash of \texttt{clean\_text} with the
first occurrence kept and the second discarded (\S\ref{sec:data}) --- a
deterministic function of the corpus, not a random draw. The
predecessor pipeline queried a hosted embedding model for its semantic
filter (\S\ref{sec:extraction}); that dependency was removed in its entirety.
The only sampling anywhere in the codebase is a draw of 20 PDFs (seed 7)
used to estimate extraction yield as a share of raw PDF text
(\S\ref{sec:extraction}); it feeds a descriptive statistic, not any column of
\texttt{results.csv}. Unlike every other quantity in this determinism claim,
this one draw is not backed by a script in the repository: no committed
file performs it, so it cannot be independently re-run from the code as it
stands, only checked against the seed value and resulting statistic
recorded here and in \S\ref{sec:extraction}. We record this rather than
imply a reproducibility this step does not have. The only stochastic
component in the repository is the LDA topic model
(\texttt{src/topic\_modeler.py}): \texttt{LdaModel.fit()} is called with
\texttt{random\_state=None} and is therefore unseeded on every run that
would produce coherence figures; only the separate stability analysis
(\texttt{compute\_stability}, gated behind \texttt{LDA\_N\_SEEDS>0} and
never invoked at the repository's current setting of
\texttt{LDA\_N\_SEEDS=0}) explicitly seeds successive runs \texttt{0}
through \texttt{n\_seeds-1}. LDA is switched off in its entirety
(\texttt{run\_lda=False}) and feeds none of \SUS, \SEN, \QUANT, \HEDGE,
\ESGSI\ or \ESGSIext. Its own
output file, \texttt{results/lda/coherence.csv}, predates the current code
and does not match its column schema, so we exclude it from every claim in
this paper rather than cite a stale artefact.

\subsection{Output schema}

\subsection*{Which artefacts correspond to which specification}

The repository carries three complete runs, and the distinction matters
because they were produced by different vocabularies and, since the
vocabulary also drives paragraph selection
(\S\ref{sec:robustness-lexicon}), by different extractions.
\texttt{results/\allowbreak metrics\_baseline\_paper/} holds the adopted
154-term specification and is the source of every figure reported in this
paper. \texttt{results/metrics/} and \texttt{results/\allowbreak
metrics\_sec/} hold the 289-term and 289\,+\,22-term robustness runs of
\S\ref{sec:robustness-lexicon}. The working tree's
\texttt{metadata/esg\_\allowbreak terms\_lemmatized.txt} is the 289-entry
expansion; the adopted 154-entry vocabulary is recoverable from the
repository history at commit \texttt{ce6804a}, and
\texttt{metadata/esg\_terms\_\allowbreak sectorial.txt} holds the 22 terms
the classification set aside. A reader reproducing the paper's numbers needs
the 154-entry list, not the working-tree file.

\medskip
Each \texttt{results.csv} is \texttt{;}-separated, 343 rows by 17
columns, listed in Table~\ref{tab:repro-schema}. The row count is already
net of the single duplicated report that deduplication removes
(\S\ref{sec:data}); nothing downstream needs to drop a row by hand. Because
\QUANT\ and \HEDGE\
have small standard deviations (0.0035 and 0.0096), recomputing \ESGSIext\
from the rounded columns reproduces it only to $\pm0.009$; recomputing from
the unrounded pipeline values is exact. The \ESGSI\ column carries no such
hazard: no document's index falls within the rounding interval of the
labelling threshold, so counting strictly positive rounded \ESGSI\ values
returns 171, exactly the number of \emph{Potential ESG-washing} labels in the
\texttt{Etiqueta} column.

\subsection{Vocabulary and hedge lexicon as published artefacts}

Against \citet{lagasio2024}, whose keyword list is attributed to GRI (2021)
and SASB (2018) but never sized, itemised or published, and whose data
availability statement reads ``No'' (\S\ref{sec:original}), every lexicon that
feeds a score in this paper is a plain-text file in the repository, one
canonical form per line, loaded by \texttt{config.py} at run time rather
than transcribed into the paper: the 154-term ESG vocabulary
(\texttt{metadata/esg\_terms.txt}, natural singular surface forms across
seven thematic blocks) and its derived lemmatised form
(\texttt{metadata/esg\_terms\_lemmatized.txt}, 85 unigrams, 62 bigrams, 7
trigrams) that the vectoriser actually consumes; the 1{,}263-form lemmatised
Loughran--McDonald hedge lexicon (\texttt{metadata/lm\_hedge\_lemmatized.txt}),
built from the Uncertainty/WeakModal/StrongModal/Constraining subset (1{,}245
of 9{,}752 rows) of the L\&M dictionary \citep{loughran2011}; and the
189-entry supplementary stopword list
(\texttt{metadata/personal\_stopwords.txt}) that participates in
preprocessing. The files a third party would need to recompute every number
in this paper are the same files that produced them.

\subsection{Four silent-failure defects, repaired}

The four defects below were found and fixed together in one commit,
\texttt{44114d0} (\emph{fix: repair ESG lexicon pipeline and restore audited
vocabulary}), but they do not share one origin. Two of them -- the \QUANT\
routing and the \HEDGE\ lemmatisation mismatch -- were introduced when
\texttt{calculate\_quant\_scores} and \texttt{calculate\_hedge\_scores} were
first written, in the same commit that retired the semantic filter for the
current deterministic pipeline (\texttt{64393f2}, \S\ref{sec:extraction}). The
other two predate that migration entirely: the alphanumeric token filter
traces to \texttt{f8bd8a5} (2026-03-21) and the vectoriser's default
\texttt{ngram\_range=(1,1)} to \texttt{fbcab9f} (2026-03-20), both about a
week before the semantic filter was even added (\texttt{813005c},
2026-03-28) and five weeks before it was retired in \texttt{64393f2}. All
four therefore survived not one refactor but the full semantic-filter era
and its retirement -- more than four months and several intervening
commits -- undetected. Every figure in Table~\ref{tab:repro-defects} is
measured on the corpus rebuilt after \texttt{44114d0}. We report them as a
methods contribution rather than as housekeeping: each produced a
plausible-looking number instead of an error, and that failure mode is the
one most likely to survive peer review in dictionary-based text
measurement.

A provenance caveat belongs here rather than left implicit. \texttt{44114d0}
is HEAD on this branch, and every commit-level claim above is checked
directly against it. It is not, on its own, sufficient to regenerate
\texttt{results.csv}: the three-specification \SUS\ system
($\susdens$, $\sustfidf$, $\suslag$ and the \texttt{config.SUS\_MODE}
switch between them), the \texttt{Breadth} column, and the deduplication
step of \S\ref{sec:repro-determinism} exist only as uncommitted changes to
\texttt{main.py}, \texttt{src/config.py} and \texttt{src/esgsi\_analyzer.py}
on top of that commit. A reader who checks out \texttt{44114d0} exactly
gets a single, non-selectable TF-IDF-mean \SUS\ score, no \texttt{Breadth}
column, and 344 undeduplicated rows -- not the pipeline this paper
describes. Closing this gap requires a further commit capturing that
working-tree state; until it exists, the commit hash names a necessary but
not sufficient condition for reproduction.

The general lesson is not specific to this codebase. In dictionary-based
measurement, a mis-specified component returns zero rather than raising an
error: a token filter, a lemmatisation mismatch or a vectoriser default can
each silently remove an entire category of matches while the pipeline runs
to completion and writes a number that looks exactly like a low-incidence
finding. The absence of a signal is therefore indistinguishable, from the
output alone, from the absence of a defect. None of the four defects in
Table~\ref{tab:repro-defects} raised an exception, a warning, or an
out-of-range value; each was found only by checking a component against text
known in advance to contain the pattern it was supposed to detect.
Components in this pipeline must therefore be, and were, validated
individually against known-positive text before their output is trusted in
any of the results that follow.
