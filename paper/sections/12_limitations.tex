with their magnitudes. This section collects them, ranks them by how much of
what we claim they constrain, points to where each is treated, and states in
full the few that no earlier section owns. Table~\ref{tab:limitations-summary}
is the index.

\subsection{What bounds every result}

\paragraph{The scores are corpus-relative and have no cardinal meaning.} Every
component of $\ESGSI$ and $\ESGSIext$ is a $z$-score over the 343 pooled
reports \eqref{eq:zscore}, so a score is a position within this corpus, in
corpus standard deviations, and a slope is in pooled standard deviations of
this sample. The sign says only on which side of the corpus mean a report
lies. No level is comparable across corpora, vocabularies or studies, and
every score, including the yearly series, would move if the panel were
extended or reduced (\S\ref{sec:index-composite}, \S\ref{sec:temporal}). The
adopted $\susdens$ estimates nothing on the sample, but the score
normalisation is cross-sectional under every specification, and the two
TF-IDF alternatives also estimate their idf weights on it
(\S\ref{sec:index-sus}). We use no threshold and classify no report
(\S\ref{sec:artefact}); this limitation is the reason, and no calibration
could remove it without an external criterion.

\paragraph{No external criterion.} Neither score is tested against an
external benchmark --- regulatory findings, controversy data or hand-coded
cases --- and no figure in this paper is evidence about any firm's conduct.
The one empirical check on the substance component is convergent validity
with $\QUANT$ (\S\ref{sec:validity}), and $\QUANT$ is internal: our regular
expressions over our own extracted zones. \S\ref{sec:validity} removes the
strings the two instruments share, after which the correlations fall by more
than a third and the firm-bootstrap interval of the difference between
$\susdens$ and $\suslag$ no longer excludes zero. The shared extraction cannot
be removed within this design, since both instruments see only the text that
\S\ref{sec:extraction} admits, so the test is conditional on that stage. The
rejection of $\suslag$ rests first on the algebra of \S\ref{sec:measurement},
which uses no criterion, and the choice of $\susdens$ over $\sustfidf$ on a
structural argument, not on evidence (\S\ref{sec:validity-adopted}).

\paragraph{The mandate and chronology confound is unresolved.} The 2018--2024
window is the one over which European non-financial reporting became
prescribed, and a firm that adds mandated tables adds ESG vocabulary without
necessarily changing anything it does. \S\ref{sec:temporal-mandate} bounds the
purely definitional part --- with the extraction held fixed, striking 22
regime names leaves an $\ESGSI$ slope of $-0.149$ and striking the transversal
pillar $-0.117$, against $-0.181$ ---
and states why no design here does better: exposure is common to all 49 firms
and collinear with calendar time. Whether the rise in substance reflects
changed conduct, compliance or unchanged conduct described in a prescribed
vocabulary is not identified. The architecture adds a further property: both
scores fall when substance rises, so a firm that complies with a new
disclosure regime moves down the scale for having complied.

\paragraph{The vocabulary was built on this corpus, and its error rate is not
reported.} Candidate terms came in part from a reading of 17 of the 343
reports, in which Germany, 98 of the reports, is represented by one firm; the
admission criteria, the collocation choices, the pillar assignment and the
sectoral designation were applied by the authors to occurrences in the same
corpus on which the scores are computed, and no part of the corpus was held
out (\S\ref{sec:lexicon-sources}, \S\ref{sec:lexicon-criteria}). The
admission criteria are judgements under stated rules, not measurements: the
paper reports no error rate for the vocabulary's matches, and the concentration
thresholds behind the sectoral designation are not in a committed script
(\S\ref{sec:lexicon-limits}). The reviewers and evaluators of the candidate
lists are not yet identified in the text; \S\ref{sec:lexicon-sources} carries
a marker for that information. The extraction parameters, the $\QUANT$
families and the set of specifications were likewise fixed with the corpus
visible. The stability checks of \S\ref{sec:robustness-lexicon} perturb this
fitted vocabulary, so the stability they show is stability around the fit
(\S\ref{sec:robustness-limits}). Every choice is disclosed
(\S\ref{sec:repro}); disclosure is not pre-commitment, and we claim only the
first.

\subsection{The vocabulary and the extraction}

\paragraph{One vocabulary selects the text and scores it.} The regular
expression that decides which paragraphs are extracted is built from the same
terms whose lemmas $\SUS$ counts (\S\ref{sec:lexicon-one}), so admitting a term
changes the text it is then counted on, and changes the text on which $\SEN$,
$\QUANT$ and $\HEDGE$ are computed. \emph{Board of directors} is the measured
case: the heaviest scoring entry, it also brings governance text into the
zones, and removing it cuts the extracted text by 6.4\,\%
(\S\ref{sec:lexicon-represent}, \S\ref{sec:robustness-lexicon}). The coupling
also limits the stability programme. Re-extraction is simulated from the
paragraphs the adopted vocabulary already extracted, which is exact in
principle for deletions only; an addition to the vocabulary, which would pull
in text that was never extracted, cannot be simulated at all, and the
single-entry deletions were run with the extraction frozen
(\S\ref{sec:robustness-lexicon}).

\paragraph{The magnitude of the trend depends on parts of the vocabulary.}
The sign of the within-firm trend survives every designed deletion, every
weight pair and all but one of 10,000 random deletions; its magnitude does
not. Without the environmental pillar the $\ESGSI$ slope falls to $-0.105$
under re-extraction, and without the transversal pillar to $-0.132$. The
environmental pillar is also the designed deletion that falls furthest
outside its matched random null: its $\rho$ lies below all but 0.2\,\% of
random deletions of the same size, because its entries order the reports
differently from the rest of the vocabulary. Under
random deletion of half the vocabulary the 5th--95th band of the slope spans
a factor of three (\S\ref{sec:robustness-lexicon},
\S\ref{sec:robustness-limits}). A statement about the size of the decline is
therefore a statement about this vocabulary.

\paragraph{Recall is bounded by the lexicon.} A term outside the extraction
regex triggers no hot paragraph and enters no zone, so it is invisible to
every component, not under-weighted (\S\ref{sec:extraction}). The 32 patterns
pull text into zones, where it feeds $\SEN$, $\QUANT$, $\HEDGE$ and the length
denominators, without ever being counted by $\SUS$. A count vocabulary cannot
carry a negative lookahead, so a polysemous head is admitted bare or only
inside enumerated collocations, losing its other valid uses
(\S\ref{sec:lexicon-criteria}); paraphrase is invisible throughout. No recall
figure exists for the vocabulary.

\paragraph{The extraction stage is untested at its free parameters.} The
density threshold (1.0 keyword per 100 words), the $\pm 1$-paragraph window
and the 150-character minimum zone were set by judgement and never varied
(\S\ref{sec:extraction}). The stage keeps under two fifths of the corpus's
full text layer (Table~\ref{tab:extraction-yield}) and sits upstream of every
number here. The navigation filter is documented by a calibration whose script
is not committed, and the pipeline does not log what the filter removes, so
its effect on the adopted extraction is unmeasured
(\S\ref{sec:extraction-nav}). Neither the sense of the vocabulary's matches nor
the filter's error rates were measured by year, so a drift in extraction
accuracy over the window is not excluded (\S\ref{sec:extraction-yield}).

\paragraph{Protected phrases are a narrow repair.} Protection covers exactly
the terms that preprocessing would empty or collapse to one word, eleven
phrases in all (\S\ref{sec:lexicon-represent}). It does not cover the other
ways preprocessing reshapes the vocabulary: two collapses (\emph{due
diligence}, \emph{eu taxonomy}) are allowed by judgement; stopword removal
lets a lemmatised n-gram match across removed words (\emph{board director} in
\emph{board and directors}, 0.3\,\% of that entry's count); and some entries
are lemmatisation artefacts rather than words (\emph{esr}, \emph{ecovadi},
\emph{sustainalytic}). The shared representation makes these harmless for
matching, since corpus and vocabulary pass through the same transformation,
but not for weighting: under $\sustfidf$ and $\suslag$ an artefact entry
carries the idf of its artefactual form. The adopted $\susdens$ weights every
entry equally and is insensitive to that.

\paragraph{PDF text-layer artefacts.} Cleaning deletes non-ASCII characters
rather than transliterating them, so a term written with a typographic hyphen
fuses (\emph{netzero}) and a word set with a ligature loses letters; the
matches lost this way are not counted, and the yield of
\S\ref{sec:extraction-yield} is biased downward by an unmeasured amount
(\S\ref{sec:extraction}). Two extracted texts carried NUL characters that
truncated them whenever the stored text was read back; the defect is repaired
and the adopted run is unaffected (Table~\ref{tab:repro-defects}), but it
shows that the text layer can carry characters no stage was designed for.

\subsection{The components and the Extended ESGSI}

\paragraph{The weights of the Extended ESGSI are a choice.} $w_q = w_h = 0.5$
is stated, not estimated, and no external criterion exists to calibrate it
(\S\ref{sec:index-composite}). Over the grid
$w_q, w_h \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1\}$ the Spearman correlation with the
adopted score is at least $0.865$, and at least $0.973$ within $0.25$ of the
adopted weights on each axis; the within-firm slope is negative in all 25
cells, but its magnitude grows roughly in proportion to $w_q$, from $-0.185$
at $w_q = 0$ to $-0.335$ at $w_q = 1$ with $w_h = 0.5$
(\S\ref{sec:robustness-weights}). The level and the rate of decline of
$\ESGSIext$ are therefore properties of the chosen weights as well as of the
reports.

\paragraph{$\QUANT$ is crude.} Seventeen vocabulary entries are also matched
by a $\QUANT$ pattern; the overlap is measured and removed in
\S\ref{sec:validity}, but it matters for $\ESGSIext$ too, which subtracts both
$\Z(\QUANT)$ and $\Z(\susdens)$, so the shared strings reinforce rather than
cancel, and their share of $\QUANT$ hits rises from 16.29\,\% in 2018 to
37.62\,\% in 2024. The \texttt{frameworks} family names reporting regimes, so
the rise of $\QUANT$ carries the chronology objection of
\S\ref{sec:temporal-mandate}. The \texttt{large\_numbers} family matches any
run of four or more digits other than a calendar year, so it counts page
numbers, note references, identifiers, share counts and monetary figures
alongside quantified ESG disclosure; it is 13{,}380 of the 186{,}984 $\QUANT$
hits (7.2\,\%), and no family was validated against ESG content. No two
components share a token base ($\SUS$ and $\SEN$ on lemmatised tokens,
$\QUANT$ on raw whitespace tokens, $\HEDGE$ on raw words;
\S\ref{sec:index-notation}), so it is score normalisation, not anything about
the quantities, that makes them addable.

\paragraph{$\HEDGE$ largely counts risk.} The Loughran--McDonald categories
are matched by form, not sense: \emph{risk} and \emph{risks} alone are
147{,}099 of the 498{,}477 hits, so in these reports $\HEDGE$ largely registers
how often risk is discussed, and at least 6{,}632 of the 38{,}628 matches of
\emph{may} are the month (\S\ref{sec:lexicon-hedge},
\S\ref{sec:index-components}). The lexicon is used as published, without
removing these. $\HEDGE$ enters only $\ESGSIext$, and it has no detectable
trend (\S\ref{sec:temporal}).

\paragraph{$\SEN$ inherits the limits of its dictionary.} Loughran--McDonald
polarity is a finance word list applied to non-financial narrative: it has no
negation or intensifier handling, an asymmetric list of 140 positive and 893
negative stems, and a bounded ratio insensitive to the volume of matched
terms --- a report with 4 positive and 2 negative matches scores exactly as
one with 400 and 200 (\S\ref{sec:index-components}). Its input is lemmatised
text that the package's tokeniser stems again. The dictionary replaces the
antecedent's TextBlob by design (\S\ref{sec:scope}), which is one of the
reasons our scores and the antecedent's are different measurements. The
absence of a trend in $\SEN$ answers only the objection that a fixed
dictionary scores later language differently (\S\ref{sec:temporal}).

\subsection{Design and inference}

\paragraph{Country and document type are confounded, and sector cells are
small.} 99 of the 101 URDs are French and all 98 German documents are annual
reports; the two effects can be separated only through 15 documents, and the
seven standalone sustainability reports belong to one firm (\S\ref{sec:data}).
No country or document-type contrast is interpretable as such, and one score
construction is applied across three document types whose comparability rests
on the extraction stage. The German subsample holds both fixed but establishes
only that a trend of the same sign exists there (\S\ref{sec:temporal}). Sector
cuts across countries and is partly informative, but industries hold 2 to 11
firms and supersectors 1 to 7, 8 of the 16 supersectors hold one or two, and
sector is identified only between firms (\S\ref{sec:data-industry}); no group
of one to three firms supports a statement about its slope
(\S\ref{sec:temporal-sectors}).

\paragraph{Inference rests on 49 clusters and seven years.} Every panel
regression clusters by firm, with $t_{48}$ references and a restricted wild
cluster bootstrap for the fixed-effects slopes (\S\ref{sec:index-inference});
with 49 clusters, and 14 in the German subsample, the asymptotic approximation
is not relied on alone. Firm clustering does not cover a shock common to all
firms in one year, and seven years cannot be clustered on, so a trend standard
error measures dispersion across firms around seven yearly means, not
uncertainty about which years were drawn. Group-slope intervals clustered by
firm are too narrow for small groups and the Wald test of equal slopes is
anti-conservative when a one-firm group is present; we read group slopes
through firm-slope intervals instead (\S\ref{sec:index-inference}). The panel
requires seven consecutive reports, so it selects large, continuously
reporting European issuers; that restricts external validity without
generating an internal trend (\S\ref{sec:temporal-mandate}).

\paragraph{The adopted run is not yet committed.} Every number is regenerated
by a script, but the adopted run depends on pipeline changes that exist only
in the working tree (rows 5--8 of Table~\ref{tab:repro-defects}), and most
analysis scripts are not yet in the repository history; the last commit does
not reproduce the adopted run (\S\ref{sec:repro-provenance}). The navigation
filter's calibration script is not committed at all
(\S\ref{sec:repro-determinism}). Until the working tree is committed, the
commit hash is a necessary, not a sufficient, condition for reproduction.

\subsection{Checks not run}

Several checks that would bear on the results were not run, and the sections
that raise them say so: variation of the extraction parameters
(\S\ref{sec:extraction}); the effect of the navigation filter on the adopted
extraction, and the accuracy of extraction by year
(\S\ref{sec:extraction-yield}); re-extraction from the PDFs rather than
simulated from extracted paragraphs, single-entry deletions under
re-extraction, and additions to the vocabulary
(\S\ref{sec:robustness-lexicon}); a clustered trend test for
$\ESGSI(\sustfidf)$ (\S\ref{sec:robustness-spec}); pillar-level trend
regressions (\S\ref{sec:temporal-pillars}); and any comparison with an
external criterion (above).
