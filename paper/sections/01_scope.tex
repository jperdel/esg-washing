\section{Scope and contribution}
\label{sec:scope}

This paper measures how the tone of a report's ESG disclosure stands relative
to the substance of that disclosure. We compute the measure for 343 reports of
49 listed firms from seven euro-area countries, one report per firm and year
over 2018--2024 (\S\ref{sec:data}). Tone is the polarity of the extracted ESG
text under the finance dictionary of \citet{loughran2011}; substance is the
density, in the same text, of mentions of a documented ESG vocabulary
(\S\ref{sec:method}).

The measure is a continuous score, and it is relative to the corpus. Each
component is standardised over the 343 reports, so a value locates a report
within this corpus and has no meaning outside it; its sign says only whether
the report lies above or below the corpus mean (\S\ref{sec:method-choices}).
The idea behind the score, positive language in excess of the substance that
supports it, fixes which direction of the score is of interest.

\paragraph{Two scores.} The main score, $\ESGSI$~\eqref{eq:esgsi}, follows the
architecture of \citet{lagasio2024}, a tone component minus a substance
component, each rescaled across the sample; we keep that architecture and
correct the specification of its parts, as listed below. The second score, the
Extended ESGSI $\ESGSIext$~\eqref{eq:esgsiext}, is the new metric this paper
proposes. It adds quantification on the side of substance and hedging on the
side of tone, with weights that are a choice rather than an estimate; we report
how far the score moves when they change (\S\ref{sec:robustness}).

\paragraph{Departures from the antecedent.} \citet{lagasio2024} computed the
index for a 2023 cross-section of 749 standalone English-language
sustainability reports. Our specification departs from it in four respects.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Corpus and extraction.} The antecedent excluded firms that
        disclose ESG information inside the annual report. A balanced panel
        needs a document that every firm publishes every year, for all but
        one of our firms the annual report or the universal registration
        document.
        Because these filings embed ESG disclosure in a much larger document,
        we score extracted ESG passages rather than whole reports
        (\S\ref{sec:method-extraction}).
  \item \emph{Substance as density.} The antecedent derives substance from
        TF-IDF weights and states neither how they are aggregated over the
        vocabulary nor whether document vectors are normalised. We count
        vocabulary mentions per 100 tokens, because under L2 vector
        normalisation, the usual default, a TF-IDF substance score does not
        register the amount of ESG content (\S\ref{sec:method-choices}).
  \item \emph{Dictionary sentiment.} Tone is measured with the
        Loughran--McDonald finance dictionary instead of the general-purpose
        TextBlob polarity used by the antecedent.
  \item \emph{$z$-scores.} The antecedent prints a difference of $z$-scores
        but describes a min--max rescaling. We use $z$-scores as the score
        normalisation of every component (\S\ref{sec:method-choices}).
\end{itemize}
Because corpus, vocabulary and sentiment dictionary all differ, our scores are
not numerically comparable with those of the antecedent, and no such comparison
is attempted.

\paragraph{Roadmap.} \S\ref{sec:data} describes the corpus and
\S\ref{sec:method} the extraction, vocabulary, scores and inference.
\S\ref{sec:results} reports the scores over time, against the confound of
reporting mandates, by firm group and by pillar; \S\ref{sec:robustness} tests
their sensitivity to the choices of the method, and \S\ref{sec:limitations}
states the limitations.
