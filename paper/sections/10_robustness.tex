\section{Robustness}
\label{sec:robustness}

We vary three choices of the method---the substance specification, the
weights of the Extended ESGSI and the vocabulary---and compare each variant
with a reference score over the same 343 reports. Every comparison uses three
measures: the Spearman correlation $\rho$ with the reference; the overlap of
the extreme deciles, that is, how many of the 34 reports with the highest
(lowest) reference score remain among the 34 highest (lowest), written
top/bottom; and the within-firm slope per year, estimated with firm fixed
effects and firm-clustered standard errors (\S\ref{sec:method-inference}).
The reference slopes are $-0.181$ for $\ESGSI$ and $-0.260$ for $\ESGSIext$
(\S\ref{sec:results-time}).

\paragraph{Substance specification.} Computing $\ESGSI$ with the
length-normalised TF-IDF measure $\sustfidf$ instead of $\susdens$ leaves its
ranking almost unchanged ($\rho = 0.993$; 33/32 of the extreme deciles kept).
With the L2 vector-normalised measure $\suslag$ the ranking changes markedly
($\rho = 0.642$; 18/21) and the slope weakens to $-0.143$. Term weighting is
thus a second-order choice and vector normalisation a first-order one
(\S\ref{sec:method-choices}).

\paragraph{Weights of the Extended ESGSI.} Over the grid
$w_q, w_h \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1\}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:esgsiext}, $\rho$
with the score at the default weights is at least 0.865, the minimum being at
$w_q = w_h = 0$, where the score is $\ESGSI$, and at least 0.973 within 0.25
of the default on each axis, where at least 28 of the top and 29 of the
bottom 34 reports remain. The ranking depends mostly on $w_h$ and the slope
mostly on $w_q$ (from $-0.185$ at $w_q = 0$ to $-0.335$ at $w_q = 1$, with
$w_h = 0.5$); the slope is negative in all 25 pairs, and every firm-clustered
95\,\% interval excludes zero.

\paragraph{Vocabulary.} The vocabulary selects the text as well as scoring it
(\S\ref{sec:method-vocab}), so each deletion of entries is run in two ways:
with the extraction held fixed, so that only the list counted by $\SUS$
changes, and with a simulated re-extraction, in which the extraction rule of
\S\ref{sec:method-extraction} is re-applied without the deleted entries and
all components are recomputed on the paragraphs that still qualify. A smaller
vocabulary can only select part of the adopted extraction, so the simulation
starts from the extracted paragraphs; with the full vocabulary it reproduces
the adopted scores ($\rho > 0.9999$). We delete designed sets of entries and,
1{,}000 times at each size, random sets of 10--50\,\% of the 434 entries, and
read each designed deletion against 1{,}000 random deletions of the same size.
Table~\ref{tab:robustness-summary} gives the re-extraction results for both
scores, and Figure~\ref{fig:robustness-stability} those for $\ESGSI$ in both
modes.

Removing the 36 sectoral entries barely moves either score ($\rho \geq 0.998$
in both modes): they carry about 1\,\% of the vocabulary's matches. The
environmental pillar moves the score most. Without it $\rho = 0.884$, only 23
of the bottom 34 reports remain, and the slope falls to $-0.105$ (SE 0.039,
firm-clustered $p = 0.009$, wild cluster bootstrap $p = 0.011$). Its $\rho$ is
lower than in 998 of the 1{,}000 random deletions of the same size, although
its entries carry no more of the matches than random sets of that size
(38.2\,\% against 38.0\,\%): the environmental entries order the reports
differently from the rest of the vocabulary. It is the only designed deletion
whose $\rho$ falls below the median for a random half of the vocabulary
(Figure~\ref{fig:robustness-stability}a). With the extraction held fixed its
effect is larger ($\rho = 0.776$, 12 of the bottom 34 kept, slope $-0.080$
with firm-clustered $p = 0.027$), because the paragraphs extracted for
environmental entries stay in the text, unscored, and dilute density. Without the transversal pillar the
slope is $-0.132$; the remaining designed deletions leave it between $-0.168$
and $-0.206$, the steepest being without pillar G, which also removes nearly a
third of the extracted text. $\ESGSIext$ moves less than $\ESGSI$, in $\rho$,
under every pillar deletion.

Under random deletion the ranking degrades smoothly: with half of the entries
removed the median $\rho$ is 0.924 for $\ESGSI$ and 0.940 for $\ESGSIext$,
and 27 of the top and 26 of the bottom 34 reports remain. The slope is the
least stable measure. Under re-extraction its median drifts towards zero as
more is deleted, from $-0.174$ at 10\,\% to $-0.118$ at 50\,\%, because less
of the original text remains; with the extraction held fixed it stays between
$-0.179$ and $-0.160$. Its sign does not change: over the 5{,}000 random
draws the $\ESGSI$ slope is negative in all but one (a 50\,\% deletion under
re-extraction), and the $\ESGSIext$ slope in all, in both modes.

These checks bound how far the scores depend on the choices varied; they do
not validate the scores (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
