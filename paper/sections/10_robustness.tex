\section{Robustness: substance specification, weights and vocabulary}
\label{sec:robustness}

This section measures how far the two scores move when the substance specification
(\S\ref{sec:robustness-spec}), the weights of the Extended ESGSI (\S\ref{sec:robustness-weights})
and the ESG vocabulary (\S\ref{sec:robustness-lexicon}) are varied, and closes with what these
checks do and do not establish (\S\ref{sec:robustness-limits}). Every comparison is between a
perturbed and a reference score over the same 343 reports, and uses three measures. The first is the Spearman rank correlation $\rho$,
with the Pearson correlation $r$ as a secondary figure. The second is the overlap of the
extreme deciles: of the 34 reports with the highest reference score, how many are also among
the 34 highest under the perturbed score, and likewise for the 34 lowest; we write it as
top/bottom counts out of 34. The third is the within-firm trend, the fixed-effects slope of
\eqref{eq:trend-fe} with firm-clustered standard errors and, where stated, the wild cluster
bootstrap $p$-value of \eqref{eq:wild-p} (\S\ref{sec:index-inference}). For the adopted scores
the slope is $-0.181$ per year for $\ESGSI$ (SE 0.035, $p_{\mathrm{cl}} = 3.7\times10^{-6}$,
$p_{\mathrm{wild}} < 0.0001$) and $-0.260$ for $\ESGSIext$ (SE 0.040,
$p_{\mathrm{cl}} = 3.7\times10^{-8}$, $p_{\mathrm{wild}} < 0.0001$; Table~\ref{tab:temporal-trend}). The section does not choose
the specification, which \S\ref{sec:justification} does on other grounds, and agreement between
two variants is not evidence that either measures substance well.

\subsection{The substance specification}
\label{sec:robustness-spec}

$\sustfidf$ share the length denominator and differ in term weighting (uniform or idf);
$\sustfidf$ and $\suslag$ share idf weights and differ in vector normalisation (division by
document length or scaling to unit L2 norm). $\BREADTH$ is reported as a diagnostic, not as a
component. Table~\ref{tab:robustness-components} gives the component correlations and
Figure~\ref{fig:robustness-specs} the three scatters that matter.

Idf weighting leaves the score's ranking almost unchanged ($\rho = 0.9926$) and keeps 33 and 32
of the 34 reports in each extreme decile. L2 vector normalisation reorders it: $\rho = 0.6421$,
and only 18 of the top 34 and 21 of the bottom 34 remain. The trend survives the substitution
but is weaker: under $\suslag$ the within-firm slope is $-0.143$ per year (SE 0.035,
$p_{\mathrm{cl}} = 1.9\times10^{-4}$, $p_{\mathrm{wild}} = 0.0004$), against $-0.181$. We did
not estimate the slope under $\sustfidf$. Each index-level correlation exceeds its
component-level counterpart ($r$ of 0.9933 against 0.9876; 0.6764 against 0.4052), because the
compared scores share the $\Z(\SEN)$ term; the component correlations are the stricter measure
of how far the substance measurements agree. Term weighting is thus a second-order choice and
vector normalisation a first-order one. The specifications are varied in $\ESGSI$ only;
$\ESGSIext$ is computed with $\susdens$ throughout.

Two things vary, and they depend on different weights. The \emph{ranking} depends mostly on
$w_h$. Holding $w_q = 0.5$, $\rho$ with the adopted score is 0.938 at $w_h = 0$ and 0.960 at
$w_h = 1$; holding $w_h = 0.5$, it is 0.967 at $w_q = 0$ and 0.986 at $w_q = 1$. The lowest
value, 0.865, is at $w_q = w_h = 0$, where the score is $\ESGSI$; the extreme deciles are
least preserved there too (22/26); every other cell keeps at least 23 of the top and 27 of the
bottom 34. Within 0.25 of the adopted weights on each axis $\rho \geq 0.973$ and at least 28
of the top and 29 of the bottom 34 remain. The Spearman correlation with $\ESGSI$ falls from 1
at $(0,0)$ to 0.701 at $(1,1)$: at the far corner of the grid $\ESGSIext$ orders the reports
markedly differently from $\ESGSI$.

The \emph{trend} depends almost only on $w_q$. At $w_h = 0.5$ the slope runs from $-0.185$
($w_q = 0$) to $-0.335$ ($w_q = 1$), about $-0.037$ per step of 0.25; at $w_q = 0.5$ it runs
from $-0.255$ ($w_h = 0$) to $-0.265$ ($w_h = 1$). $\QUANT$ rises over the panel and $\HEDGE$
does not (\S\ref{sec:temporal}), so subtracting more of $\Z(\QUANT)$ steepens the decline and
adding more of $\Z(\HEDGE)$ barely moves it. The slope is negative in all 25 cells, with
firm-clustered standard errors between 0.032 and 0.052; the largest $p_{\mathrm{cl}}$,
$3.7\times10^{-6}$, is that of $\ESGSI$ itself, and every 95\,\% interval excludes zero. The
sign of the trend is therefore not a product of the weights; its magnitude in $\ESGSIext$ is,
roughly in proportion to $w_q$.

\subsection{Stability of the vocabulary}
\label{sec:robustness-lexicon}

The standing objection to a dictionary measure is that the result belongs to the word list. We
perturb our vocabulary of \S\ref{sec:lexicon} in designed ways and at random, and read
every designed deletion against random deletions of the same size.

\paragraph{Frozen extraction and simulated re-extraction.} The vocabulary selects the text as
well as scoring it (\S\ref{sec:lexicon-one}), so deleting entries changes both. Every
perturbation is therefore run in two modes. In the \emph{frozen} mode the extracted text is that
of the adopted run and only the list that $\susdens$ counts changes. This isolates the scoring
effect, but paragraphs that were extracted only because of the deleted entries stay in the
text, now unscored, and dilute the density of the reports that discuss those topics most. In
the \emph{re-extraction} mode the extraction rule of \S\ref{sec:extraction} is re-applied
without the deleted entries, paragraphs that no longer qualify drop out, and $\SUS$, $\SEN$,
$\QUANT$ and $\HEDGE$ are recomputed on what remains, as a real change of vocabulary would do.

Re-extraction is simulated from the extracted paragraphs, not from the PDFs. A subset of the
vocabulary can only make fewer paragraphs hot, so the text it would extract is a subset of the
adopted extraction, and re-applying the rule (hot paragraphs, one context paragraph on each
side, merging, the 150-character minimum) to the extracted paragraphs reproduces it. Three
approximations remain: each paragraph is lemmatised on its own rather than within its
document; a shorter entry nested in a deleted one (\emph{carbon} in \emph{carbon footprint}) is
not re-checked as a trigger, which slightly overstates the text lost; and $\QUANT$ and
$\HEDGE$ are counted per paragraph. With the full vocabulary the simulation re-selects every
extracted paragraph and reproduces the pipeline's components at $r = 0.99995$ ($\SUS$),
$0.999999$ ($\SEN$), $0.99994$ ($\QUANT$) and $0.99997$ ($\HEDGE$); $\ESGSI$ at $r = 0.99997$
and $\rho = 0.99991$ (largest absolute difference 0.091), $\ESGSIext$ at $r = 0.99998$ and
$\rho = 0.99995$; its slopes are $-0.180$ and $-0.260$. In both modes each perturbed score is
compared with the simulation's own full-vocabulary score.

\nolinkurl{scripts/stability_v3_e2e.py} runs both modes over the 434 source entries (402 terms,
32 patterns; deleting a pattern changes nothing in the frozen mode).
\nolinkurl{scripts/stability_v3.py} runs the frozen mode over the 399 lemmatised entries against
the pipeline's own scores and adds single-entry deletion; where the two overlap they agree
(without pillar E, frozen $\rho$ 0.7785 against 0.7761). \nolinkurl{scripts/robustness_v3.py}
adds firm-clustered and wild-bootstrap inference for the designed deletions.

\emph{Sectoral entries.} Excluding them, the sensitivity that \S\ref{sec:lexicon} announces,
leaves $\ESGSI$ at $\rho = 0.9995$ under re-extraction (0.9987 frozen), keeps 33 of each
extreme decile (all 34 frozen) and the slope at $-0.176$ ($-0.184$ frozen). No random deletion
of 36 entries moves the score as little. That is a matter of mass, not of harmlessness: in the
frozen programme the 34 sectoral lemmas hold 1.0\,\% of vocabulary hits, against 8.5\,\% on
average for 34 lemmas drawn at random.

\emph{Entries admitted with a caveat.} Removing the six gives $\rho = 0.9912$, keeps 32 of each
extreme decile and leaves the slope at $-0.180$ ($-0.195$ frozen). They move the score more
than 99\,\% of random six-entry deletions for the mirror reason: the five lemmatised ones hold
9.3\,\% of vocabulary hits, against 1.3\,\% for five random lemmas.

\emph{Pillars.} The environmental pillar matters most. Without it $\rho = 0.8844$ under
re-extraction, 28 of the top 34 remain but only 23 of the bottom 34, and the slope falls to
$-0.105$ per year (SE 0.039, $p_{\mathrm{cl}} = 0.009$, $p_{\mathrm{wild}} = 0.011$), about 58\,\%
of its full-vocabulary value; frozen, $-0.080$ ($p_{\mathrm{cl}} = 0.027$,
$p_{\mathrm{wild}} = 0.031$). Unlike the two previous sets, E is not heavy for its size (its
lemmas hold 38.2\,\% of hits, against 38.0\,\% for 152 random lemmas), yet its $\rho$ lies below
all but 0.2\,\% of matched random deletions: the environmental entries order the reports
differently from the rest of the vocabulary, and the reports they order are chiefly those at the
bottom of the score, the ones whose substance is high relative to their tone. Without TRANS the
slope is $-0.132$ (SE 0.036, $p_{\mathrm{cl}} = 5.5\times10^{-4}$, $p_{\mathrm{wild}} = 0.0006$) at
$\rho = 0.9822$; these two pillars are the ones that change the trend's magnitude. Pillar G
removes the most text (31.4\,\% of extracted words) and lowers $\rho$ to 0.9439, below all but
0.4\,\% of the null, while steepening the slope to $-0.206$; its lemmas hold 30.0\,\% of hits
against 22.6\,\% for a random set of the same size. Pillar S is the exception in the other
direction: $\rho = 0.9836$, above 97\,\% of the null, because it holds 17.6\,\% of hits against
31.7\,\%.

\emph{Board of directors.} The heaviest entry (\S\ref{sec:lexicon-represent},
Table~\ref{tab:lexicon-mass}) is also an extraction trigger: removing it cuts the extracted text
by 6.4\,\%. Under re-extraction $\rho = 0.9953$, with 33 and 31 of the extreme deciles kept, and
the slope steepens slightly to $-0.188$; frozen, $\rho = 0.9846$ and the slope is $-0.194$ (SE
0.034, $p_{\mathrm{cl}} = 7.4\times10^{-7}$, $p_{\mathrm{wild}} < 0.0001$). One random entry
holds 0.24\,\% of hits on average against its 5.7\,\%, so its percentile (0.5) says only that it
is heavy.

Apart from pillars E and TRANS, every designed deletion leaves the $\ESGSI$ slope with
$p_{\mathrm{cl}} < 10^{-4}$ in both modes; every designed deletion, E and TRANS included, leaves
the $\ESGSIext$ slope so. $\ESGSIext$ moves less than $\ESGSI$ under
each pillar deletion, as $\Z(\QUANT)$ and $\Z(\HEDGE)$ depend on the vocabulary only through the
extracted text: without E its $\rho$ is 0.9088 and its slope $-0.211$
($p_{\mathrm{cl}} = 6.5\times10^{-7}$).

\emph{Frozen against re-extracted.} The modes agree except for pillar E, where freezing the
text exaggerates the effect ($\rho$ 0.7761 against 0.8844; 12 of the bottom 34 kept against 23)
through the dilution described above, and \emph{board of directors}, where re-extraction also
removes the governance text the entry brought into the zones.

The slope is the least stable of the three measures (panel c), and its bands are wide. Under
re-extraction the median drifts towards zero as more is deleted, from $-0.174$ at 10\,\% to

\subsection{What the checks establish}
\label{sec:robustness-limits}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The vocabulary was built on this corpus.} Candidates, admission criteria and the
  sectoral designation were all applied to the 343 reports (\S\ref{sec:lexicon-limits}). The
  perturbations are of a vocabulary fitted to the sample, not of an independent one, and the
  stability they show is stability around that fit.
\item \emph{High correlations are partly structural.} A deletion leaves the retained entries
  common to both scores, so the correlation has a floor. With $t$ the ratio of the standard
  deviations of the per-report densities of the deleted and the retained entries, the
  component-level floor is $\sqrt{\max(0, 1-t^2)}$, carried to the index through the common
  $\Z(\SEN)$ term (frozen text). The sectoral deletion ($t = 0.053$) gives $r = 0.9993$ against
  a floor of 0.9992, \emph{board of directors} ($t = 0.192$) 0.9902 against 0.9900, pillar G
  0.9456 against 0.9449; pillar E ($t = 1.261$) 0.8064 against 0.4446. A high correlation after
  a small deletion records how little the deleted entries weigh, which is why each deletion is
  read against random deletions of the same size.
\item \emph{Stability is not validity.} The checks bound how far the scores depend on the
  choices varied. Every designed deletion except pillar E leaves $\rho \geq 0.935$ and at least
  27 of each extreme decile; the sign of the within-firm trend survives every designed
  deletion, every weight pair and all but one of the 10,000 random draws. The trend's magnitude
  is less secure: it depends on the environmental and TRANS entries, on how
  much of the vocabulary is kept, and in $\ESGSIext$ on $w_q$. None of this shows that the
  vocabulary measures substance; that question belongs to \S\ref{sec:lexicon-limits} and
  \S\ref{sec:validity}.
\end{enumerate}
