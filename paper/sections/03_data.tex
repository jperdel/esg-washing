\section{Data and corpus construction}
\label{sec:data}

The corpus comprises 343 unique corporate reports from 49 listed companies
headquartered in seven European countries (Belgium, Finland, France,
Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Spain), one report per company per year
from 2018 to 2024. The panel is exactly balanced: every year contributes 49
documents, and each country contributes the same number of documents in every
year (its firm count in Table~\ref{tab:data-country}). Balance is temporal,
not cross-national: countries are represented in very unequal numbers, but no
year departs compositionally from any other. Firms enter the paper only
through their country, their industry and anonymous positions; no firm is
named.

Three document types are represented: annual reports, the Universal
Registration Document (URD; French \emph{document d'enregistrement
universel}), and standalone sustainability reports. Document type was
assigned per report from an external metadata file
(\nolinkurl{metadata/muestras_informes.xlsx}), joined to each PDF by country,
publication year and a normalised company name --- Unicode-folded,
punctuation-stripped, and reconciled through a manual company-name map
covering divergences between the directory tree and the metadata source. The
join resolved all 343 documents; no case was left unmatched.

\subsection{The country/type confound is a structural property of the corpus}

Table~\ref{tab:data-country} reports the corpus by country and document
type. Document type is confounded with country almost completely: 99 of the
corpus's 101 URD filings are French, and all 98 German documents are annual
reports. Both are properties of national reporting practice rather than
sampling accidents --- the URD is the standard annual regulatory filing for
French listed companies and routinely carries the extended sustainability
disclosure, whereas the German issuers in this sample report ESG content
inside the ordinary annual report. We state this here as a design property
of the corpus: within the pooled panel, a country effect and an
annual-report/URD effect can be told apart only through 15 documents (the 13
French annual reports and the 2 Dutch URDs), and the seven standalone
sustainability reports all come from one French firm. Germany, Italy, Spain,
Finland and Belgium are each a single country and a single document type (all
annual reports); Germany, with 14 firms and 98 documents, is the largest such
slice, and \S\ref{sec:temporal} reports a within-Germany regression for that
reason.

\subsection{Industry composition}
\label{sec:data-industry}

Benchmark (ICB). The classification was supplied by the authors as a
firm-level metadata file (\nolinkurl{metadata/empresas_supersector.csv}), keyed
on the same company names as the directory tree; it is not derived from the
reports. The 49 firms fall into 16 supersectors. For analysis we also group
supersectors into their ICB industries, the level above in the ICB hierarchy:
10 of ICB's 11 industries are represented, Real Estate being the exception.
Table~\ref{tab:data-industry} gives both levels. The counts in
Tables~\ref{tab:data-country}, \ref{tab:data-year}
and~\ref{tab:data-industry}, and those quoted around them, are produced by
\nolinkurl{scripts/corpus_composition.py} from the document list of the
adopted run; they do not depend on the vocabulary.

\paragraph{What the sector dimension can identify.} Sector cuts across
countries: every industry has firms in at least two countries, 13 of the 16
supersectors do as well, and the two countries with most firms each span
seven industries (France's 17 firms and Germany's 14). Industry contrasts can
therefore be drawn within a single country and document regime, which a
country contrast cannot, and \S\ref{sec:temporal-sectors} draws them within
France and within Germany for that reason.

\paragraph{What it cannot identify.} Three limits bound it. First, cells are
small: industries hold 2 to 11 firms and supersectors 1 to 7, and 8 of the 16
supersectors hold one or two firms, so a group-level interval rests on very
few degrees of freedom, or none (\S\ref{sec:index-inference}). Second, sector
is constant within a firm, so it is identified only from the 49 firms, never
from within-firm variation, and it is absorbed by firm fixed effects. Third,
sector does not break the country/type confound where the confound is
sharpest: the 16 firms that file any URD are 15 of the 17 French firms and one
Dutch firm, so an industry's URD share is essentially its French share, and
the seven standalone sustainability reports belong to a single Health Care
firm. Some industries sit in a narrow set of countries (Utilities in Italy and
Spain, Technology in Germany and the Netherlands), and a contrast between them
is also a contrast between those countries.

Firms are assigned to countries as in the directory tree and in the metadata
workbook. One assignment is open to argument: one firm is incorporated in the
Netherlands but formed from predecessors headquartered elsewhere, and is
classified under the Netherlands, consistently with both sources. It is also
the Dutch firm whose filings include the two non-French URDs (5 annual reports
and 2 URDs). Reclassifying it would move seven documents out of the
Netherlands and would leave its industry unchanged.

\subsection{Comparability across document types}

The panel spans three document types with different structure and
different length: annual reports in which ESG disclosure is one section
among many financial and operational ones, URD filings that combine
regulatory financial, governance and non-financial statements, and
standalone sustainability reports that are ESG content throughout. Raw
comparability across types cannot be assumed. Every measurement from
\S\ref{sec:extraction} onward accordingly operates not on whole documents but
on the ESG-relevant zones the lexical filter identifies within each one,
which is what lets a single score construction (\S\ref{sec:index}) be applied
uniformly across all three types. It does not make the types equivalent: the
extraction is applied uniformly, not validated separately by type, and
because type is sorted by country, no document-type effect on either score
is identified (\S\ref{sec:temporal-sectors}).

\subsection{Deduplication}

Corpus assembly began from 344 collected PDF filings, one more than the
final corpus size. The extra file is one 2019 report present twice, the
second time as a \texttt{\_repaired} re-issue of the same filing under a
different file name. A content-hash comparison identified this single
duplicate pair. The hash is taken over the preprocessed (lemmatised)
representation of \S\ref{sec:lexicon} rather than the raw extraction, so the
criterion is that two documents are indistinguishable to every downstream
measure; here the two files are in fact identical at the extraction stage as
well. The \texttt{\_repaired} copy was removed as a data-quality control,
leaving 343 unique reports. The balanced panel reported above --- 49
documents in every year, and a fixed per-country count within every year ---
holds only after this step: before deduplication, 2019 carried 50 documents,
with the Netherlands contributing 7 rather than 6, while every other year
already stood at 49.
