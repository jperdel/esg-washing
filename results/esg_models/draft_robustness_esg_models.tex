Open classifiers that cover the whole text point the same way. FinBERT's
sentence-level tone \citep{huang2023finbert}, on a random sample of 200 passages
per report, correlates with $\Z(\SEN)$ at $\rho = 0.77$ ($[0.68, 0.84]$; within
firms $r = 0.73$), and also within environmental, social and governance passages
($0.66$, $0.69$ and $0.41$). Pillar shares from FinBERT-ESG and ESGBERT
\citep{schimanski2024esgbert} track ours ($\rho = 0.83$ for governance, $0.88$
and $0.70$ for the environmental and social pillars), and both reproduce the fall
of the governance share between 2018 and 2024. The 2024 change in tone has two
parts. The fall of $\Z(\SEN)$ sits in the disclosure-topic words of the ESRS
tables and vanishes without them, but both FinBERT and ClimateBERT also find fewer
positive statements in environmental and social narrative ($-0.27$ standard
deviations for FinBERT, unchanged without those words), which the dictionary does
not register. In a re-read of 98 reports, at most 8\,\% of the text that ESGBERT
labels as ESG lies outside the extraction.
